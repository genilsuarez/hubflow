Past Tenses

Past simple against past continuous: which one moves the story forward, which one sets the scene, and the time markers that give each away.

Simple vs continuous Interrupted actions Time markers State verbs

⏰ Simple vs Continuous

Past simplePast continuous
Formworked / wentwas / were + -ing
Sees the action ascomplete, a single pointin progress, unfinished
Job in a storymoves events forwardsets the background
ExampleI read the report.I was reading the report.

The same facts, framed differently: She cooked dinner says it got done. She was cooking dinner says we are looking at the middle of it, and something else is probably coming.

⚡ The Interruption Pattern

The long action goes in the continuous, the short one that cuts in goes in the simple.
while + continuous
While I was walking home, it started to rain.
when + simple
I was walking home when it started to rain.
Two parallel backgrounds
She was cooking while he was setting the table.
Two events in sequence
He opened the door and walked out.
Both simple is not the same sentence. When I arrived, she left = she left after I got there. When I arrived, she was leaving = she was already on her way out.

📅 Time Markers

Points toMarkers
Past simpleyesterday · last week · in 2019 · two days ago · then · after that
Past continuouswhile · at 8 o'clock · all morning · at that moment
Either (different meaning)when
A finished time expression blocks the present perfect. With yesterday or last year you must use the past simple: I saw her yesterday, never I have seen her yesterday.

🧠 State Verbs Don't Take -ing

Verbs of thinking, feeling, belonging and the senses describe a state, not an activity.
I knew the answer. / She had a car.
I was knowing the answer. / She was having a car.

Common state verbs: know, believe, understand, want, need, like, love, hate, own, belong, seem, cost, mean. Some switch to an activity meaning and then do take -ing: She was having lunch (= eating), He was thinking about it (= considering).
