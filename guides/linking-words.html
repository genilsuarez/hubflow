Reference Guide

Linking Words & Connectors

The glue that turns a list of sentences into a paragraph that flows — organised by what job each connector does.

➕ Addition

⊕
Adding another point
Reinforce or stack onto a previous idea — usually more formal than "also" or "and"
moreover furthermore in addition also besides what is more not only… but also
Moreover, the new system is 40% faster. • In addition, membership is affordable. • Not only is it cheap, but it also works offline.

⇆ Contrast

↔
Signalling the opposite
Introduce a counter-point, concession, or unexpected result
however nevertheless nonetheless on the other hand whereas although / though even though despite / in spite of
She studied hard. However, she still failed. • Although it was expensive, we bought it. • Despite the rain, we went for a walk.

💡 Cause

→
Explaining why
Introduce the reason behind a statement or situation
because since as due to owing to on account of
Since traffic was heavy, we arrived late. • The flight was cancelled due to bad weather. • Because she was tired, she went to bed early.
⚠️ due to vs because
due to and owing to take a noun phrase — not a full clause. because takes subject + verb.

➡ Result

🔷
What happened next
Introduce the outcome or consequence of a previous statement
therefore as a result consequently hence thus so
It rained all night. As a result, the streets flooded. • He didn't prepare. Consequently, he failed the exam.

🎯 Purpose

🎯
Expressing intent or goal
Show what something is meant to achieve
so that in order to to (+ infinitive) in order that lest
She spoke slowly so that everyone could understand. • He trained hard in order to make the team.
