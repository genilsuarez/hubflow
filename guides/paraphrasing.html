Paraphrasing & Summary

Rewriting a sentence without losing its meaning: the five transformation moves, the key-word task, and how to compress a text into a summary.

Five moves Key word transformation Summarising

🔧 Five Transformation Moves

1 · Change word class
Verb ↔ noun ↔ adjective.
They decided quickly.
→ They made a quick decision.
2 · Change voice
Active ↔ passive shifts the focus.
The committee rejected it.
→ It was rejected by the committee.
3 · Use a synonym
Match register and strength, not just meaning.
The plan failed.
→ The plan was unsuccessful.
4 · Restructure the clause
Swap the connector and reorder.
Although it rained, we went.
→ We went despite the rain.
5 · Change the negation
Say the opposite of the opposite.
It's not expensive.
→ It's quite cheap.
A paraphrase keeps the meaning, including its strength. Some students struggled → a few students found it difficult is fine; → students failed is a different claim.

🔑 Key Word Transformation

Rewrite the sentence using the given word, unchanged, within the allowed number of words.
  1. Identify what the key word forces. despite needs a noun or -ing; although needs a clause.
  2. Find the grammar point being tested — passive, reported speech, conditional, inversion, causative.
  3. Build around the key word first, then attach the rest.
  4. Count the words. Contractions count as two (didn't = did not).
  5. Never change the key word — no plural, no tense, no derivation.
OriginalKey wordTransformation
It's too expensive for me to buy.AFFORDI can't afford to buy it.
Someone stole my bike.HADI had my bike stolen.
"I'll call you," she said.WOULDShe said she would call me.
I regret not studying more.WISHI wish I had studied more.
The last time I saw him was in May.SINCEI haven't seen him since May.

📜 Summarising

A summary is a shorter text with the same shape — not a list of the sentences you liked.
  1. Find the main claim of each paragraph, usually its first or last sentence.
  2. Delete examples, repetition and detail. They support the claim; they are not the claim.
  3. Generalise lists — buses, trams and trains → public transport.
  4. Keep the logical relations — if the original contrasts two positions, the summary must contrast them too.
  5. Use your own structures. Copying three or more consecutive words is quotation, not summary.
FunctionUseful phrases
OpenThe text argues that… · The author examines…
AddIt goes on to… · Furthermore, the writer notes…
ContrastIn contrast, … · The article then qualifies this by…
CloseIt concludes that… · Overall, the piece maintains that…
Report, don't join in. A summary carries the author's position, not yours. Keep reporting verbs neutral unless the task asks for evaluation.
