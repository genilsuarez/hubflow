Punctuation Cheat Sheet

Small marks, big consequences — the comma, apostrophe and capital letter rules that trip up even confident writers.

📌 Commas

Lists of three or more
Separate each item with a comma. The comma before "and" (the Oxford comma) is optional but recommended for clarity.
apples, oranges, and bananas
After an introductory phrase
A comma separates an opening word or phrase (time, condition, linking word) from the main clause.
After the meeting, we went to lunch. However, the results were disappointing.
Non-defining relative clauses
Extra, non-essential information about a noun gets commas on both sides.
My brother, who lives in Canada, is visiting.
Joining two independent clauses
Use a comma before and / but / so / or when both sides could be complete sentences on their own.
She was tired, but she kept working.

' Apostrophes

💡 Its vs it's
It's = "it is" or "it has". Its (no apostrophe) = possessive, like "his" or "her".
It's a beautiful day. The dog wagged its tail.
Possessives
Singular nouns add 's. Regular plural nouns just add an apostrophe after the s. Irregular plurals (like "children") add 's as usual.
the dog's bone the students' books the children's toys

📡 Capitalization

Always capitalize
The pronoun I, proper nouns (names, cities, countries), and days/months are always capitalized in English — unlike Spanish.
I visited Paris on Sunday.
