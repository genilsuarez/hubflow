Relative Clauses

Relative pronouns add extra information about a noun without starting a new sentence.

PronounRefers toExample
whoPeople (subject)The man who called...
whichThingsThe book which is on the table...
whosePossessionThe woman whose car was stolen...
wherePlacesThe house where I grew up...
whenTimesThe day when we met...

🎓 Advanced Relative Clauses

Beyond who/which/that: the defining–non-defining split, prepositions, and quantifier relatives.
DefiningNon-defining

The students who passed will get certificates. (only some passed)
The students, who passed, will get certificates. (all of them passed)
StructureExample
preposition + which/whomthe report on which the decision was based
quantifier + of whom/whichforty applicants, most of whom were rejected
whose + nouna company whose shares collapsed
which = the whole clauseHe resigned, which surprised everyone.
reduced relativethe man standing at the door
Sentential which needs a comma and refers back to the entire preceding clause — the one case where which has no single noun antecedent.

✂ Participle Clauses

A non-finite clause that compresses a full clause into a phrase. Formal and written by default.
FormMeaningExample
-ing (present)active, simultaneous or causalFeeling tired, she went to bed.
-ed (past)passiveWritten in 1890, the novel still sells.
having + participlecompleted before the main verbHaving finished, he left.
conjunction + -ingthe relation made explicitAfter leaving school, she travelled.
not + participlenegativeNot knowing the way, we asked.
Dangling participle: the participle's subject must be the subject of the main clause. Walking home, the rain started says the rain was walking. Fix it: Walking home, I got caught in the rain.
