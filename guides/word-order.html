Word Order Untangled

English word order is far stricter than Spanish — here's the skeleton every sentence hangs on.

🏗 Basic Word Order — SVOMPT

Subject · Verb · Object · Manner · Place · Time

She drank coffee quickly at the café this morning.
💡 Unlike Spanish
English rarely moves the subject or object for style — the order is fixed. Time and place can sometimes move to the front for emphasis, but manner almost never does.
This morning she drank coffee quickly at the café. ✓ Coffee she drank quickly. ✗

⌛ Adverb Placement

Frequency adverbs
Before the main verb
always · often · usually · never · rarely · sometimes
She always drinks coffee.
He never arrives late.
After "to be"
Adverb comes after be
is · are · was · were · been
She is always late.
They are usually quiet.

🔄 Negative Fronting & Inversion

❗ Negative adverb at start = inversion
Putting never, rarely, hardly, seldom, or not only at the front forces question-style word order for emphasis.
Never have I seen such a mess. Rarely does she complain. Not only did he lie, but he also stole. Never I have seen such a mess. ✗

❓ Question Word Order

Statement
Subject + Aux/Verb + …
You do exercise often.
She has finished the report.
Yes/No Question
Aux + Subject + Verb + …
Do you exercise often?
Has she finished the report?
Wh- Question
Wh- + Aux + Subject + Verb
How often do you exercise?
Where has she gone?
Subject Question
Wh- + Verb (no inversion!)
Who called you? (not "who did call")
What happened?
