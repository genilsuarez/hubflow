🧩 How a Text Is Organised

Every paragraph does a job. Naming the job is how you read fast and write clearly.
MoveSignals
Frame the topicIn recent years… · The question of X has…
State the claimThis article argues… · It can be shown that…
ElaborateThat is to say… · In other words…
ExemplifyFor instance… · A case in point is…
ConcedeAdmittedly… · While it is true that…
CounterHowever… · Nevertheless… · That said…
ConcludeOn balance… · The evidence therefore points to…
Theme position carries emphasis. Whatever stands first in a sentence is what the sentence is about. Starting successive sentences with the same element is what makes a paragraph feel coherent.

⚖️ Building an Argument

Claim
What you assert. One sentence, no hedging yet.
Grounds
Data, examples, sources. Concrete.
Warrant
Why the grounds support the claim — the step most writers skip.
Qualifier
The scope: in most cases, where funding allows.
Rebuttal
The strongest objection, stated fairly and answered.
Restatement
The claim again, now narrowed by everything above.
Weak moveStronger move
Everyone knows that…Survey data from 2024 indicate that…
Opponents are simply wrong.This objection holds only if we assume…

X causes Y.X is strongly associated with Y, though causality remains unclear.
Concession is not weakness. While the cost argument has force, it overlooks… reads as stronger than ignoring the objection, because it shows you have tested your own claim.

💭 Idiom in Context

An idiom's dictionary meaning is only half of it; the other half is where it is allowed to appear.
IdiomMeaningFits
a blessing in disguisebad at first, good laterneutral, spoken or written
to bite the bulletaccept something unpleasantinformal to neutral
the elephant in the roomthe obvious issue nobody namesbusiness, journalism
to move the goalpostschange the criteria unfairlycriticism, negative tone
by the same tokenfor the same reasonformal, argumentative
to cut cornersskip steps to save timealways disapproving
Idioms are fixed. Spill the beans — not spill the peas; on cloud nine — not on cloud eight. Changing a word does not soften an idiom, it breaks it.

Test three things before using one: does the register match, is the attitude (approving or critical) the one you intend, and does the surrounding grammar let the idiom keep its fixed form?
