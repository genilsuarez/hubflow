✍️ Filling a Gap

A gap is never solved by the gap. It is solved by the two or three words on either side.
  1. Read the whole text first. Later sentences often reveal the topic that decides an earlier gap.
  2. Decide the word class. After the comes a noun; after very comes an adjective; after a subject comes a verb.
  3. Use the grammar signals — a plural verb needs a plural subject; since forces a perfect tense.
  4. Check collocation. English fixes many pairs: make a decision, heavy rain, deeply concerned.
  5. Re-read the sentence with your answer in it. If it sounds like something no one would say, it is wrong.

Watch the referring words. it, this, they, such and the former point back to something already mentioned. If your answer makes that reference impossible, reconsider.

🧭 Matching Meaning

Work from the answers you are sure of; each one you place shrinks the field.
Anchor first
Place the two or three obvious pairs, then reconsider what is left.
Use word parts
-tion = a noun, -ful = an adjective, un- / dis- = a negative meaning.
Match the register
A formal definition usually belongs to a formal word: to terminate → to end officially, not to stop.
Beware near-synonyms
Two options may both fit loosely. The right one matches the whole definition, including its intensity.
