Adverbs in Place

Adverbs of frequency and manner: how they are formed, and the three positions an English adverb is allowed to occupy.

Frequency Manner Position

🔄 Adverbs of Frequency

AdverbRoughlyExample
always100%He always arrives early.
usually90%We usually eat at home.
often70%She often works late.
sometimes50%I sometimes walk to work.
rarely / seldom10%They rarely complain.
never0%He never drinks coffee.
never is already negative — I never go, not I don't never go.

Longer expressions (every day, once a week, twice a month, from time to time) behave differently: they go at the end of the sentence, not in mid-position.

🎭 Adverbs of Manner

Most are the adjective plus -ly, with four spelling rules and a handful of exceptions.

She works hard. / He plays well.
She works hardly. / He plays good.

After be, seem, look, feel, sound, taste and smell, use the adjective, not the adverb: It smells good. She looks tired.

📏 The Three Positions

PositionWhere exactlyUsed byExample
Frontbefore the subjectcomment, timeSuddenly, the lights went out.
Midafter be, before other main verbs, after the first auxiliaryfrequency, certaintyShe often works late.
Endafter the objectmanner, place, timeHe drove carefully.
She is always late. / She has always been late.
She always is late. / She always has been late.
Never split verb and object. I speak English well — the adverb waits until the object is done. This is the single most common adverb error at A2/B1.
