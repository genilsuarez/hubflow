🔍 The Recurring Errors

Nine patterns account for most of what a proofreader marks.
ErrorWrongRight
Subject-verb agreementHe go to work.He goes to work.
Double pastDid you went?Did you go?
Double negativeI don't know nothing.I don't know anything.
Article missingI am teacher.I am a teacher.
Plural after numberthree bookthree books
Prepositiondepend ofdepend on
Adjective / adverbShe sings beautiful.She sings beautifully.
Word orderI like very much pizza.I like pizza very much.
Run-onIt rained, we stayed in.It rained, so we stayed in.

🇪🇸 Traps Specific to Spanish Speakers

Subject dropped
Spanish allows it; English needs a subject in every clause.
✗ Is raining.
✓ It is raining.
People + singular verb
People is already plural.
✗ People is nice.
✓ People are nice.
Information / advice
Uncountable — no plural, no an.
✗ informations, an advice
✓ some information, a piece of advice
Adjective agreement
English adjectives never take -s.
✗ reds cars
✓ red cars
for vs since
for + duration, since + starting point.
✗ since two years
✓ for two years / since 2024
Explain me
explain, say and suggest take to.
✗ Explain me the rule.
✓ Explain the rule to me.

✅ A Fixed Checking Order

Scan for one thing at a time. Reading "for mistakes" in general finds fewer than five focused passes.
  1. Verb — does it agree with its subject, and is the tense right?
  2. Nouns — singular or plural, countable or not, article present?
  3. Small words — prepositions, a/the, this/these.
  4. Word class — should that adjective be an adverb?
  5. Order and punctuation — adverb position, comma splices, capitals.
Read it aloud. Most errors that survive a silent read are audible: a missing -s, a doubled negative, a comma splice that leaves you out of breath.
