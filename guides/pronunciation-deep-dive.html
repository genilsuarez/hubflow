Natural Speech & Listening

How words blend, rise and fall in real conversation — connected speech, intonation, vowels, clusters, and the listening ear you need to catch it all.

🔗 Connected Speech & Linking

In fast, natural speech, words blend into each other. These reductions and links are everywhere in spoken English — even though we rarely write them this way.

💪 Content words vs function words
Nouns, main verbs, adjectives and adverbs carry the stress — articles, prepositions and pronouns stay weak and fast. I BOUGHT a NEW CAR today. English is stress-timed: the time between stressed syllables stays roughly equal, so unstressed syllables get squeezed in between.

🔤 Consonant Clusters & -ED Endings

English stacks consonants in ways Spanish doesn't — and the -ED ending never adds an extra syllable unless the verb already ends in /t/ or /d/.

🛣️/str/
3 consonants before a vowel
street, strong, struggle
🌸/spr/
lips move from /s/ to rounded /r/
spring, spray, sprout
3️⃣/θr/
tongue between teeth, then /r/
three, throw, threat
💰/sts/
3 final consonants — no extra vowel
costs, lists, tests
🧠/ŋk/
the "n" is actually /ŋ/
think, bank, drink
🥤/dr/
often sounds like /dʒr/ — "jrink"
drink, dream, drive
-ED Ending: 3 Sounds, Never a New Rule
/t/
after voiceless p, k, f, s, sh, ch
walked, helped, washed
/d/
after vowels & voiced consonants
played, called, lived
/ɪd/
only after /t/ or /d/ — extra syllable
wanted, needed, started

🎧 Listening: Catching Near-Homophones

Most listening mistakes aren't about vocabulary — they're about two words that sound almost identical. Train your ear on these traps first.

🐑Ship / Sheep
/ɪ/ vs /iː/ — short vs long
I can see the ship on the horizon.
🍰Three / Free / Tree
/θ/ vs /f/ vs /t/
He ate three pieces of cake.
🏜️Desert / Dessert
stress moves: DES-ert vs des-SERT
The desert is very hot and dry.
👜Employees / Employers
same root, opposite meaning
Our department is hiring new employees.

✍️ Spelling by Ear: -ing / -ed

Once you can hear a verb clearly, the spelling follows the same rules every time: double the final consonant, drop the silent -e, or just add the ending.

🏃run → running
CVC word: double the final consonant
run + n + ing
✍️make → making
silent -e: drop it before -ing
mak(e) + ing
😵die → dying
-ie ending: ie → y
d(ie→y) + ing
🎮play → playing
just add -ing, no change
play + ing

📈 Sentence Stress

English is stress-timed: stressed syllables arrive at roughly even intervals and everything between them is compressed.
Usually stressedUsually unstressed
nouns, main verbs, adjectives, adverbsarticles, prepositions, pronouns
negatives (don't, never)auxiliaries (is, have, do)
wh-words, demonstrativesconjunctions (and, but, that)

Both of these take about the same time to say: DOGS eat MEAT and the DOGS have EATen the MEAT. The extra syllables are squeezed, not added to the length.

🔗 Linking Basics

Words are not separated in speech. Three linking types cover most cases.
Consonant → vowel
The consonant attaches to the next word.
an apple → a-napple
turn it off → tur-ni-toff
Intrusive /r/, /w/, /j/
A glide appears between two vowels.
law and order → law-r-and
go out → go-w-out
I am → I-y-am
Same consonant
Pronounced once, held longer.
big game → bi-game
bus stop → bu-stop

🎯 Contrastive Stress

Moving the stress changes what the sentence denies, without changing a single word.
Stressed wordImplication
I didn't say she stole it.Someone else did.
I didn't say she stole it.I implied it.
I didn't say she stole it.I meant someone else.
I didn't say she stole it.She borrowed it.
I didn't say she stole it.She stole something else.

Contrastive stress overrides every other rule: even the, of or a can take the nucleus when they are what is being corrected — I said a report, not the report.

✏️ Thought Groups & Chunking

Speech is delivered in short groups, each with one main stress and its own tone.
Where groups break
At clause boundaries, before connectors, around inserted phrases, and at commas.
When I got home | the lights were off | so I called Ana.
One nucleus each
Each group carries a single strongest syllable, usually on the last content word.
…the lights were OFF…
Chunking is a comprehension tool too. Listening for the pauses tells you where the clauses are, which is often enough to parse a sentence whose individual words you missed.

💬 Discourse Marker Prosody

Markers such as however, actually and you know sit in their own tone group — that is how listeners know they are signposts, not content.
MarkerTypical deliveryFunction
However,separate group, slight rise, pausecontrast ahead

Well,lengthened, levelhesitation, partial disagreement
So,low onset, pause afternew topic or conclusion

Never stress a marker's content meaning. Saying ACTually with full lexical stress turns a soft correction into a contradiction.

🌎 Accent Variation

Beyond the two reference accents, a handful of features explain most of what you hear.
AccentGive-away feature

Australian/eɪ/ → /aɪ/ (day ≈ die), non-rhotic, high rising terminal

Train comprehension, not imitation. The goal is to understand these accents when you meet them; your own production only needs to be consistent and clear.
