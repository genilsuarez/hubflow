Reference Guide

Text Cohesion

The small words that stop a text from repeating itself — and what exactly they point back to.

📌 Reference Words

it
↑ points back to
one specific thing already named
I bought a phone. It is very fast.
this
↑ points back to
a whole previous idea or situation
The team lost. This surprised everyone.
these
↑ points back to
two or more things mentioned before
She listed problems. These were serious.
the former
the latter
↑ points back to
first / second of two options (formal)
Two jobs: the former paid more, the latter was closer.

🔄 Substitution Words

so
Replaces a whole clause — after think / hope / believe / say
Is he coming? I hope so.
She thinks so too.
one / ones
Replaces a singular (or plural) countable noun already mentioned
I need a pen — do you have one?
I'll take the red ones.
either / neither
either agrees with negatives; neither = not one nor the other
I don't like it. My brother doesn't either.
Neither of them called.
do so
Replaces a verb phrase to avoid repetition (formal)
She was asked to leave, and she did so immediately.

✏️ Ellipsis

💡 Leaving words out
Ellipsis omits words that are already understood from context. Common in conversation and informal writing.
"Can you come tomorrow?" — "Yes, I can come tomorrow." → Yes, I can.
"Are you coming?" — "Hoping to."  (= I'm hoping to come)
⚡ Cohesion vs Coherence
Cohesion = the surface links (pronouns, connectors, substitution). Coherence = the underlying logic that makes a text make sense even if every sentence stands alone. Both matter in writing — but cohesion is the part you can directly teach and test.
