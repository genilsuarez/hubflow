Future Forms

English has no future tense — it has six ways of talking about later, and choosing between them is about evidence, planning and arrangement.

The six forms will vs going to Predictions Time clauses

🔮 The Six Forms

FormMeaningExample
will + basedecision made now, prediction, promiseI'll help you.
be going toprior intention, or evidence you can seeShe's going to resign.
present continuousfixed arrangement with another personWe're meeting at six.
present simpletimetable, scheduleThe train leaves at 9.
future continuousin progress at a future pointThis time tomorrow I'll be flying.
future perfectfinished before a future pointBy June I'll have graduated.

⚖️ will vs going to

will — decided now
The decision happens as you speak.
— We've run out of milk.
— OK, I'll get some.
going to — decided before
The plan already existed.
— Why the shopping bag?
— I'm going to buy milk.
will — belief
Prediction from opinion or experience.
I think it will rain later.
going to — evidence
Prediction from what you can see now.
Look at those clouds — it's going to rain.
Offers, promises, refusals and requests all take will: I'll carry that. I won't tell anyone. Will you hold this? Here going to would sound like a pre-existing plan.

📈 Grading a Prediction

ConfidenceLanguage
Near certainwill definitely · is bound to · is certain to
Likelywill probably · is likely to · I expect
Possiblemay · might · could · there's a chance
Unlikelyprobably won't · is unlikely to
Near impossibledefinitely won't · there's no way

Note the position of the adverb: will probably come but probably won't come. In the negative the adverb moves in front.

⏳ No will After a Time Word

When I get home, I'll call you.
When I will get home, I'll call you.

After when, as soon as, before, after, until, once and if, English uses a present form even though the meaning is future. Use the present perfect when the sequence matters: Once I've finished, we'll eat.
