Asking Questions

Yes/no questions, wh-questions, the subject-question exception, and question tags — with the inversion rule that governs all four.

Yes / No Wh- questions Subject questions Question tags

❓ Yes / No Questions

Move the auxiliary in front of the subject. If there is no auxiliary, borrow do.
StatementQuestionWhy
She is ready.Is she ready?be inverts on its own
They can swim.Can they swim?modal inverts
He has finished.Has he finished?perfect auxiliary inverts
You work here.Do you work here?no auxiliary → add do
She works here.Does she work here?does takes the -s
They went home.Did they go home?did takes the past
The tense marker moves only once. Once does or did is there, the main verb returns to its base form: Did she go? — never Did she went?

🔍 Wh- Questions

Wh-word first, then exactly the same inversion as a yes/no question.
WordAsks aboutExample
whopersonWho did you call?
whatthingWhat are you reading?
whereplaceWhere does he live?
whentimeWhen did it start?
whyreasonWhy are you laughing?
howmannerHow did you get here?
how much / manyquantityHow many people came?
whosepossessionWhose bag is this?
Stranded prepositions: everyday English leaves the preposition at the end — Who are you waiting for? What is it made of? Fronting it (For whom are you waiting?) is correct but formal.
Do you know where he lives?
Do you know where does he live?

An indirect question — one buried inside another sentence — goes back to statement order. Only the outer question inverts.

🎯 Subject Questions: the No-do Exception

When the wh-word is the subject, nothing inverts and do never appears.
Who broke the window? — who is the subject
Who did break the window?
TypeQuestionAnswer
SubjectWho called you?Ana called me.
ObjectWho did you call?I called Ana.
SubjectWhat happened?An accident happened.
ObjectWhat did you see?I saw an accident.
Quick test: answer the question. If the answer replaces the wh-word at the start of the sentence, it is a subject question — leave do out.

💬 Question Tags

Opposite polarity, same auxiliary, pronoun subject.
StatementTagRule
You're coming,aren't you?positive → negative
She doesn't smoke,does she?negative → positive
They went home,didn't they?no auxiliary → do
I'm late,aren't I?irregular by convention
Let's go,shall we?fixed form
Open the door,will you?imperative → will
Intonation carries the meaning. Falling tone = you expect agreement (Nice day, isn't it?↘). Rising tone = you are genuinely asking (You locked the door, didn't you?↗).
